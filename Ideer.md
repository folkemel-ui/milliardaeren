# Ideer

The idea list for a business-empire game inspired by *Business Empire: RichMan*. Nothing is set in stone. We build in packs of 3–4 items at a time. When an item is done it is removed from this list and the list is renumbered; the git log holds the history. Refer to items by title, not number.

Stack: React + Vite + TypeScript, pure seeded engine (no `Math.random`/`Date.now` in the engine), Norwegian UI, NOK, no new dependencies.

## Pack plan

A suggested order, grouped so each pack feels complete when played. The order is a suggestion; packs can be swapped or reshuffled at any time. Items are referred to by title.

**Next up on the game track: Pack 125 – A manager who learns.** The first of three manager packs from the list on 20 October. Today (Pack 124) the manager keeps one of three fixed blends every Monday for 0.2 % a week (0.34 % an hour) and sees nothing of the market; with 1 mrd for 12 game hours it gave Forsiktig +11–16 %, Balansert +14–20 %, Offensiv +21–36 % against the savings account’s +9.4 %, with 41–48 mill in fees. (1) **A better manager over time** — steps bought once: a lower fee, then the judgement below, then more frequent rebalancing; underneath a step on `s.forvalter` (optional, no save version), priced on what it manages or on net worth. (2) **Follows the business cycle** — stocks drift +3 % an hour in a boom and −4 % in a recession, gold the opposite, phases of 28 days that the game shows with the days left; the manager moves a share of the blend between stocks and gold/bonds by phase. (3) **Times the bonds to the interest rate** — the savings account floats with the policy rate (1 % an hour × rate / 4) while a bond locks it (2-year +10 %, 10-year +25 % on top): 10-year bonds when the rate is 5 %, the savings account when it is 2.5 %, 2-year in normal times. Underneath, the manager’s bond post gets a third place to be (the savings account), which needs `settInnSparing`/`trekkFraSparing` so the cost price follows. (4) **Rebalancing more often** — daily, or the day a post is more than 3 % off, for a higher fee. The bot never uses the manager, so the golden master and the bench stay. Questions at the start: the steps and their prices (e.g. three steps at 1 %, 3 % and 10 % of what it manages, or fixed sums by net worth), how far the cycle tilts the blend (e.g. Balansert’s stocks 25 % → 35 % in a boom and 15 % in a recession, gold the other way), whether the bond timing uses the savings account or only the two bonds, and what more frequent rebalancing costs (e.g. 0.3 % a week instead of 0.2).

### Working side by side

- **Two folders, two branches** (Pack 63). The game track works in `Desktop\New folder (3)` on branch `spill`; the graphics track in `Desktop\milliardaer-grafikk` on branch `grafikk` — a git worktree of the same repo, with its own `node_modules`. ## A smarter manager

From the manager list on 20 October 2026: Folke picked 10 of 15 (the other five are under *Parked*). Packed as 125–127 (the judgement and the levels, the purposes, then the numbers). The manager today (Pack 124, `forvalter.ts`): three fixed blends (Forsiktig, Balansert, Offensiv), brought back every Monday morning with a 3 % slack, a flat fee of 0.2 % a week of what it manages — 0.34 % an hour, also on the bonds, which earn ~1.1 % an hour — always the 2-year bonds, the stocks equal-weighted by risk, and it sees nothing of the market. Measured with 1 mrd for 12 game hours in three games: the savings account +9.4 %, Forsiktig +11–16 %, Balansert +14–20 %, Offensiv +21–36 %, with 41–48 mill in fees.

### The manager’s judgement

1. **Follows the business cycle.** The blend is the same in every phase, yet stocks drift +3 % an hour in a boom and −4 % in a recession, gold the opposite, and the game shows the phase and the days left (28 days, 2 h 20 min). The manager tilts towards stocks in a boom and towards gold and bonds in a recession. A player can do the same by hand, so it is judgement, not free money.
2. **Times the bonds to the interest rate.** It always buys the 2-year bonds. A bond locks today’s rate (5 % in a boom, 2.5 % in a recession), while the savings account floats with it: long bonds when the rate is high, the savings account when it is low.
3. **An income profile.** The low-risk stocks pay ~0.07–0.085 % a day in dividends (~0.6–0.7 % an hour on open days). A fourth profile, «Utbytte», holds them and pays the dividends out as cash.
4. **Tax-aware rebalancing.** Every rebalance sells winners, and gains are taxed with the month at up to 28 %. Rebalance with new money first, and sell only when a post is far off.
5. **Rebalancing more often.** Daily, or the day a post drifts more than 3 % off its target, for a slightly higher fee.

### Working for you

6. **Regular deposits.** A setting like the savings share from Pack 124: «Gi forvalteren 10 / 25 / 50 % av inntekten», every second or every Monday, so the portfolio grows without pressing buttons.
7. **A savings goal.** «Spar til neste bedrift» (e.g. Rederi at 25 mrd): the manager invests towards the price, safer as the goal comes closer, and pays out when it is reached.
8. **A better manager over time.** Upgrades in steps, for a one-off price: a lower fee, then the business cycle (1) and the bonds by the rate (2), then rebalancing more often (5).

### Seeing it work

9. **Gain after fees, against the savings account.** «+kr 148 mill siden start, kr 44 mill i gebyr — kr 52 mill mer enn sparekontoen» on the card, so you see whether it pays.
10. **A line in the Sunday paper and a curve.** «Forvalteren ga +3,1 % denne uka (sparekontoen +2,5 %)» in «Uka di», and the managed value as a curve on the card.

**Game ideas**
- **Take your group public.** List your own group on the exchange with its own ticker: sell shares to raise money, the price follows your quarterly results, and the shareholders can be unhappy.
- **A finish line.** Reaching #1 on the Forbes list or kr 1 trillion gives a proper ending: a closing screen and a front page in the paper. Then choose to keep playing or start over.
- **Start-over bonus.** Sell everything for "legacy points" that give a permanent bonus in the next game.
- **Random events with choices.** Crashes, booms, strikes, scandals and inspections, each with two or three ways to respond. Drawn from a hash, not the die.
- **Rivals that fight back.** Today the rivals grow and can be taken over, but never come after you. Let them bid against you for landmarks, try a hostile takeover of one of your businesses, or poach your staff.
- **Daily and weekly missions.** Short goals with small rewards, so a five-minute visit has a point too.
- **Temporary boosts.** Marketing campaigns for ×2 income for a limited time, followed by a cooldown.
- **Offline income report.** A "while you were away" screen with a Claim button and an hour cap that managers can raise.
- **Auction house.** Rare cars and art sold at auctions with rising bids.
- **Sound.** Synthesized coin and level-up sounds using Web Audio.
- **Other sports clubs.** Hockey and basketball, in addition to football.

**Expansions of what exists**
- **Opening hours and rush hours.** The kiosk earns most in the evening, the café in the morning and the restaurant at dinner, using the game clock.

- **Credit rating.** AAA to C based on how you handle debt; affects interest and credit limit.
- **Mortgages.** Borrow against specific properties at a better rate.
- **Building your own.** Buy a plot and build over several in-game days: cheaper, but ties up money.
- **Holiday home effect.** The cabin and island give status, and weekend rental income.
- **Car value that changes.** Classic cars rise in value, new cars lose value.
