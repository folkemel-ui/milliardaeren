# Ideer

The idea list for a business-empire game inspired by *Business Empire: RichMan*. Nothing is set in stone. We build in packs of 3–4 items at a time. When an item is done it is removed from this list and the list is renumbered; the git log holds the history. Refer to items by title, not number.

Stack: React + Vite + TypeScript, pure seeded engine (no `Math.random`/`Date.now` in the engine), Norwegian UI, NOK, no new dependencies.

## Pack plan

A suggested order, grouped so each pack feels complete when played. The order is a suggestion; packs can be swapped or reshuffled at any time. Items are referred to by title.

Version 1.0 was reached with Pack 43.

Order across the tracks: Pack 63 gave each track its own folder and branch and split the stylesheet by owner, so the two tracks run side by side. G12 put the property and luxury drawings, the maps and the gallery outside the start-up script, so new drawings land in pieces that load when shown.

**Next up on the game track: Pack 92 – A farm you build.** The farms (`engine/jord.ts`) are two farms (Hedmarken 40 mill, Lista 150 mill) and two forests, each owned or not: a farm pays a harvest every Monday of 8 % of the land’s value in a normal week (~14 %/h), moved by the week’s weather (×0.4–1.6), and the only choice is buy or sell — all four are bought by hour ~4, and late in the game a harvest is seconds of income. Four items make a farm something you build: (1) **More fields** — each farm starts with 3 and grows to 10, each new one ×1.5 the last, and the harvest follows the fields; (2) **The field map** — the farm’s page shows its fields as tiles (what grows, how far it has come, where the next one goes), and a tap buys or tends one; (3) **Machines** — a tractor (+10 % harvest), a combine (+15 %) and a sprayer (−30 % weather loss), in steps like a business’s improvements; (4) **The farm grows in stages** — småbruk → gård → storgård → gods as fields are added, with status at each stage. The prices follow the farm’s value so it grows with the player. The fields, machines and stages need drawings (Graphics item 15), and G18 — which draws the two farms in the full frame — has not landed yet (the graphics track is at G17), so the field map can start as plain tiles and the scene wait for G18. Questions at the start: what a field and each machine cost (a share of the farm’s value, or fixed prices), how big the harvest grows from 3 to 10 fields (linear, or less for each), what each stage gives (status, a bonus, a name on the card), and whether the forests get fields too or stay as they are.

**Next up on the graphics track: Pack G18 – A full frame: buildings, land and landmarks.** G17 is done (the eleven homes), so the full frame goes on with thirteen drawings: the office buildings in Bjørvika and at Forus, the shopping centre in Trondheim, Aker Brygge, the private island in Lofoten, the farms on Hedmarken (Hamar) and Lista (Farsund), the forests in Trysil and Namdalen (Namsos), Ytterskjær lighthouse, Kollen hoppbakke (Holmenkollen), Steinvik borg and Oslotårnet (Bjørvika). All are at far distance; the island, the lighthouse and the castle stand on the sea (an irregular shore front, G7) and the ski hill and the forests fill the bottom of the canvas (`Bunnfade`), so those must reach −40 and 136 themselves. Same method as G17: the ids go in `FULL_RAMME`, the sides are drawn, and the card tile gets a square crop in `FLISUTSNITT` (the farms, forests and landmarks use the tile on `JordOgLandemerker` too, so they pick it up as it is). The drawings live in `ved-behov/Eiendomstegninger.tsx` (114 kB raw and 27.1 gzipped after G17), so the start-up script does not grow from them — and the start-up script (249.9 kB gzipped after G17; Pack 74 took it from 248.7 to 249.8) now has a budget of 500 kB, doubled on 11 October 2026 so the game can grow. A helper a business also needs still goes in the start file; the rest stays in the part, since a phone reads the whole start-up script before the first screen. Questions at the start: what each place continues into at the sides, named per drawing from `sted` (Bjørvika: the Opera and the fjord; Forus: a business park with car parks and roundabouts; Hamar: fields to Mjøsa; Lista: a flat coast with wind-bent trees; Namsos: a river and a sawmill; Holmenkollen: the city and the fjord below), and whether the three at sea get more sea (islets, boats) or a coast.

The work runs in **two tracks, each in its own session**. They are built side by side in the same repo, so they must not step on each other (see *Working side by side* below).

## The farms

Asked for after Pack 76: «make the farms more intricate — when you open a farm there should be an option to buy another patch or another tractor, so it works more like a farming simulator.» The farms today (`engine/jord.ts`): two farms (Hedmarken 40 mill, Lista 150 mill) and two forests (Trysil 60 mill, Namdalen 250 mill), each owned or not. A farm pays a harvest every Monday of 8 % of the land’s value in a normal week (a game week is 35 minutes, ~14 %/h), moved by the week’s weather between ~0.4 and 1.6; the only choice is buy or sell. Land rises 0.3 % a game day. All four cost ~0.5 mrd and are done by hour ~4, and late in the game a harvest is seconds of income — so upgrades are priced as a share of the farm’s value and the farm grows with the player.

1. **More fields.** Each farm starts with 3 fields and can grow to 10, each new one ×1.5 the last; the harvest follows the fields, so a farm becomes something you build, like a business with levels.
2. **The field map.** The farm’s page shows its fields as tiles: what grows, how far it has come and where the next field could go; a tap on a tile buys or tends it. This is the farm’s menu.
3. **Machines.** A tractor (+10 % harvest), a combine harvester (+15 %), a sprayer (−30 % weather loss), in steps like a business’s improvements, drawn in the farm’s scene as they are bought.
4. **The farm grows in stages.** Småbruk → gård → storgård → gods as fields and buildings are added, visible in the drawing, with status at each stage.
5. **Irrigation and drainage.** Irrigation takes most of the loss in a dry, sunny week, drainage most of it in a wet one: two upgrades, each answering one kind of weather.
6. **A greenhouse.** One field under glass, free of the weather and growing all year (tomatoes, cucumbers): steady, lower income than the open fields.
7. **Crop prices that move.** Grain, potatoes and fruit get a weekly price from a hash, like the trends in Pack 49, so when you sell matters.
8. **A silo and a barn.** Keep the grain instead of selling it on Monday, and sell when the price is high (item 7); the silo’s size sets how much can be held back.
9. **Animals.** A barn with spaces: cows give milk every day, hens eggs, sheep wool in the autumn, pigs meat twice a year — a steady daily income against the swinging crops, and animals in the scene.
10. **Farm support.** Norwegian production support paid per field and per animal at each month’s end: a small, steady income and a touch of real Norway (a new `totalt*` counter — wisdom.md §5).
11. **The farm’s own accounts.** Harvest, animals, support and costs per week on the farm’s page, so you see what the farm earns and what each upgrade added.

## Safe saves and the store

From the technical review: a build measured into a scratch folder, the rest read in the code.

1. **A save that stays.** Safari deletes a site’s storage after 7 days without a visit (players in a Safari tab, not on the home screen); there is no `navigator.storage.persist()` and no hint to add the game to the home screen, and `skrivTilDisk` (`state/lager.ts`) swallows a full storage without a word, with up to four copies of ~178 kB side by side. Call `persist()` at start, show Safari players a one-time «Legg spillet på Hjem-skjermen» card, and on a full storage delete the rescue copies, try again and warn once.
2. **A crash report you can send.** The crash screen (`Avbrudd.tsx`) shows the message and offers the backup, but no version, stack or copy; a «fra en nyere spillversjon» error offers a new game where an update and reload is the answer; errors on a tap show nothing. «Kopier feilrapport» (version, stack, user agent, the save as an `MLD0:` code), the save from before the last update under Innstillinger, an update-and-reload for the newer-version case, and a toast for errors on a tap.
3. **Copying that works on an iPhone.** «Kopier kode» and «Lagre som fil» copy or share only after an awaited gzip (`FlyttSpillet.tsx:19, 32`), which Safari usually refuses; in a store wrapper `a.download` does nothing. Build the code when the panel opens so the copy runs at once (or give `ClipboardItem` a promise). Check it in the phone test first.
4. **A first start that works offline.** The service worker registers on `load` (`pwa.ts:9`) and caches only the shell at install (`sw.js:36`), so the start script, CSS and font are not stored until the second online visit; offline after that the coin breathes forever, and page loads go network-first with no time limit (`sw.js:63–75`). Cache every `assets/…` at install, race the network against ~3 s, and show «Kunne ikke starte – Prøv igjen» if the app has not drawn after 10 s.
5. **A smaller font.** `InterVariable.woff2` is 352 kB — more than the whole start script gzipped — with every script in it and no preload. A Latin subset (Latin-1 and « » ‹ › – — · × ’), about a quarter of the size, with `<link rel="preload">`.
6. **The start script without the other tabs.** It is 261.6 kB gzipped (863.9 kB raw); every tab is in it, though the game opens on Bedrifter (the club 44 kB of source, the wordmarks 12 kB gzipped, the rival portraits 32 kB of source). Load the club, Investeringer/Avisa and the portraits through `vedBehov`, as G12 did: an estimated 40–60 kB gzipped less.
7. **A cache that keeps what is used.** `beskjaerHurtiglager` (`sw.js:124`) keeps the 40 newest entries and never re-stores a cache hit, so files that never change (the font, the map pieces) are the first thrown out while in use, and every `index.html?utgave=…` check stays cached. Re-store on a hit (least recently used goes first) and don’t cache requests with a query string.
8. **Gold you can read in the light theme.** The gold `#a67c00` as text is 3.50:1 on the background and 3.82:1 on cards (50 rules use `var(--gull)`), and white on the gold button 3.82:1 — under 4.5:1. `#8a6700` gives 4.79, 4.50 and 5.22. (Accessibility as a feature was declined before v1.0; this is only the colour.)
9. **What a store build needs.** A 1024 × 1024 icon without transparency (the biggest today is 512), a padded maskable icon (the star sits 199 px from the centre against a 204.8 px safe zone), `id`, `screenshots` and `categories` in the manifest, and in the native build a save kept in a file and mirrored, since iOS can wipe a web view’s storage when the device is short of space.

- **Selling businesses.** Sell to a buyer at a price based on income, with bids that vary.
- **Limit orders.** "Buy NLT if the price falls to 450"; a first step toward auto-trading.
- **Watchlist.** Star the stocks and coins you follow so they show at the top.
- **Crypto events.** Listing on a big exchange, hacked exchange, and "rug pull" for the smallest coins.
- **Short selling.** Bet against a stock; risky and requires a loan.
- **Credit rating.** AAA to C based on how you handle debt; affects interest and credit limit.
- **Mortgages.** Borrow against specific properties at a better rate.
- **Building your own.** Buy a plot and build over several in-game days: cheaper, but ties up money.
- **Holiday home effect.** The cabin and island give status, and weekend rental income.
- **Car value that changes.** Classic cars rise in value, new cars lose value.
- **Using your items.** A boat trip on Sunday for status that day, a jet to "a meeting".
- **Collection bonuses.** All the cars gives "Car collector", all the watches "Watch nerd", and so on.
- **Events you can attend.** Charity gala, yacht race, opera premiere: cost money, give status, appear in the paper.
- **Paper ads.** Offers in the paper, for example "Cabin for sale at 20 % discount, today only".
- **Rewards for achievements.** Small permanent bonuses or cash rewards.
- **Titles for your largest business.** "Lemonade king", "Sausage baron" and so on, shown on Profile.

**Map ideas**
- **Businesses on the map.** Your kiosks, cafés and so on appear as small icons in the cities, so the map shows your whole empire.
- **Rivals on the map.** Each rival has a color; cities where they own landmarks or businesses are marked.
- **Deals on the map.** Markers pop up now and then, like "Plot for sale in Trysil, 20 % off today", and disappear after a while.
- **Local events.** "Festival in Bergen this week, +30 % rent", shown as a flag on the city and mentioned in the paper.
- **Fog over what's locked.** Cities you can't buy in yet are hidden in fog that clears as your net worth grows.

**Layout ideas**
- **Colorful cartoon style.** Bright colors, rounded shapes and big icons.
- **Notification badges** on tabs when something needs attention.
- **Progress bar per business** that fills up and pays out when full.
- **Floating "+kr" numbers** on payouts.
